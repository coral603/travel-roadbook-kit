# Route decision framework

## Make options structurally different

Change at least one major dimension:

- geographic scope;
- loop versus one-way route;
- single rental versus segmented rentals;
- rail/flight/ferry substitution;
- number of bases;
- flagship hike or scenic-road priority.

Do not present three options that visit the same places in a slightly different order.

## Compare on shared criteria

Score only after explaining the evidence. Suggested criteria:

| Criterion | What to inspect |
|---|---|
| Experience fit | Must-dos, landscape variety, signature experiences |
| Transit burden | Door-to-door time, packing cycles, long days |
| Robustness | Weather fallbacks, schedule frequency, missed-connection impact |
| Cost exposure | One-way fees, flights, ferries, parking, cancellation risk |
| Booking difficulty | Scarce rooms, seasonal transport, limited vehicle supply |
| Group fit | Drivers, luggage, hiking ability, preferred pace |

Use low/medium/high or a 1–5 scale only when inputs are comparable. Explain the decisive trade-off in plain language.

## Recommendation standard

A recommendation must include:

1. why it best matches stated priorities;
2. what the group gives up;
3. the one fact that could reverse the recommendation;
4. the next decision or verification needed.

Separate:

- confirmed facts;
- live prices or schedules checked on a stated date;
- reasonable estimates;
- personal judgement.
